Mini Biography: Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Known as the "Missile Man of India" and the "People's President,"

Indian aerospace scientist and statesman

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 to 27 July 2015) was an eminent Indian aerospace scientist and statesman who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Revered across the nation as the "Missile Man of India" and the "People's President," he dedicated his life to advancing India’s scientific capabilities and inspiring millions of young minds through his humility, wisdom, and vision for a developed nation.

Early Life

Born into a humble Tamil Muslim family in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam learned the values of hard work, honesty, and discipline from a young age, even distributing newspapers to support his family’s income. Despite modest financial means, he excelled in his studies, graduating in physics from Saint Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, before pursuing aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology (MIT). His childhood fascination with flight and encouragement from inspiring teachers laid the foundation for his remarkable career in science and technology.

Importent Achivements

Kalam spent over four decades as a scientist and science administrator, primarily at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). At ISRO, he served as the project director of India’s first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III), which successfully deployed the Rohini satellite in near-Earth orbit in 1980. Beyond defense and space, Kalam also applied his scientific expertise to affordable healthcare, collaborating with cardiologist Soma Raju to develop the low-cost Kalam-Raju Stent in 1998 and a rugged tablet computer for rural healthcare workers in 2012
